In Kolkata, the BJP organised two Tiranga Yatras on Monday against what it described as violence during the NEET protests. Senior leaders, including ministers Agnimitra Paul and Tapas Ray, led marches that retraced the route taken by CJP demonstrators on July 24, when clashes at Dorina Crossing led to at least 16 arrests (the_hindu). Posters at the BJP marches depicted cockroaches being eaten by lizards with slogans such as “Beware of disguised cockroaches” and “We will crush the cockroach gang and build the nation,” alleging foreign funding for the CJP movement (the_hindu). A separate rally by journalists in Kolkata protested attacks on media personnel during the July 24 demonstration; police said at least six FIRs had been registered in connection with those assaults (the_hindu).

## 3. Madras High Court Cancels Government Jobs for Karur Stampede Victims' Families

*A Division Bench of the Madurai Bench struck down the government orders following a batch of public interest litigation petitions challenging the decision.*

> REPORT  ·  3 sources  ·  6 claim(s) kept  ·  6 verified

### Context

The Madras High Court has struck down government orders that granted state jobs to the families of 41 people killed in a stampede at a Tamilaga Vettri Kazhagam (TVK) rally addressed by party president Vijay in September 2025. A Division Bench comprising Justices C.V. Karthikeyan and R. Sakthivel delivered the ruling, overturning the decision by Chief Minister Vijay's government to provide the appointments. The court had previously allowed the temporary distribution of appointment orders subject to the outcome of the judicial review.

### Report

The Madurai Bench of the Madras High Court on Monday struck down government orders that had granted state jobs to the families of 41 people killed in a stampede at a Tamilaga Vettri Kazhagam rally in Karur last September, ruling that the appointments violated constitutional guarantees of equality and equal opportunity in public employment (the_hindu).

A Division Bench of Justices C.V. Karthikeyan and R. Sakthivel allowed a batch of public interest litigation petitions challenging the decision by the Chief Minister Vijay-led government. The court held that the orders were contrary to Articles 14 and 16 of the Constitution, emphasising that executive compassion cannot override constitutional safeguards (indian_express).

The judgment came less than three weeks after the same Bench had permitted the government to distribute appointment orders at an event in Karur, but only on a temporary basis subject to judicial review. At that time, the judges had described judicial interference in a policy decision as “extremely narrow,” while insisting the appointments would remain provisional until the legal challenge was decided and before the recipients drew their first salary (indian_express).

In its final ruling, the court rejected the state’s argument that the jobs were a legitimate exercise of executive power under Article 162. “If executive action were to be left unfettered and given free hand, chaos will reign,” the Bench observed, adding that any exercise of executive authority must remain within constitutional limits (the_hindu).

The judges noted that compassionate appointments are already governed by established rules and waiting lists across government departments for families of employees who die in service. Extending the benefit outside that framework, the court said, would unfairly overlook those already awaiting consideration under existing schemes. “There are many who wait seeking employment,” the Bench observed, holding that public jobs “are not to be thrown away by the state” but must be earned through the constitutional framework governing recruitment (indian_express).

The court warned that permitting such appointments would inevitably open the door to similar demands following other tragedies, including industrial accidents, fireworks explosions, and motor accidents where victims had received ex gratia compensation but not government employment (indian_express). “The grant of such compassionate employment to those affected in this incident would only open the floodgates for others,” the judges said, referring specifically to fireworks accidents and road transport accidents due to negligence (the_hindu).

Instead of offering public jobs, the court suggested the government could have invested in technical education, entrepreneurship, and skill development for the affected families. “The government would be creating leaders, entrepreneurs, self-sufficient individuals,” the Bench observed, adding that the State could bear the cost of such professional training (indian_express).

The stampede occurred in September 2025 during a TVK roadshow addressed by Vijay, who is also the party president (the_hindu). The litigation was initiated by Madurai lawyer Theeran Thirumurugan, among others, challenging the government’s decision to bypass standard recruitment procedures (indian_express).

**Sources:** Indian Express, The Hindu

The flooding has drawn significant attention from national media outlets covering the developing humanitarian and environmental impact.

"We had a television, a cupboard and a sofa. Nothing was left. We couldn't even save a glass to drink water from," she told BBC Hindi from a relief camp (google_news_india).

Patro’s village floods every year, but residents across the three worst-hit districts — Sivasagar, Charaideo and Jorhat — say they have never experienced inundation of this scale in their lifetimes (indian_express). Floodwaters reached critical levels on July 19 and have spread daily since, submerging entire villages, vast agricultural tracts, and major towns such as Sivasagar under water well above chest level (indian_express). More than 1.15 lakh people are in relief camps, while the National Disaster Response Force, Army, State Disaster Response Force, Fire & Emergency Services and local administrations have evacuated around 4,455 people by boat (indian_express).

A scientist at the Meteorological Centre Kohima, however, told The Indian Express the area recorded 137 mm of rain over eight to nine hours — heavy rainfall, but not a cloudburst, which the India Meteorological Department defines as more than 100 mm in one hour (indian_express). That spell also triggered landslides in Mon that killed nine people (indian_express). Chief Minister Himanta Biswa Sarma said districts in Nagaland and Assam received rainfall between 435% and 490% above normal over the period, causing rivers to rise at unprecedented speed (google_news_india). As of Friday, the tributaries Dikhow in Sivasagar, Disang at Nanglamoraghat and Dhansiri at Numaligarh were all flowing at "severe flood situation" levels (indian_express).

Relief operations are hampered by the aftermath of the deluge. Sarma said Friday the administration had yet to reach around 30% of areas in Nazira Assembly constituency and 20% in Sivasagar constituency, both in Sivasagar district, even after water levels receded (indian_express). Receding waters left behind layers of sediment several feet high, making access by foot or vehicle difficult (indian_express). The government has yet to reach more than 50,000 people for relief (indian_express). Chief Secretary Ravi Kota has deployed officials to worst-hit districts to speed up relief and restoration, prioritising supply of materials and restoration of essential services (google_news_india).

Assam lies on the vast floodplain of the Brahmaputra, fed by more than 50 tributaries descending from the Himalayas. Nearly 40% of the state is classified as flood-prone by the National Flood Commission (google_news_india). During the monsoon, heavy rain swells the river; as it crosses the flat plains it deposits sand and silt, reducing carrying capacity and making flooding more likely. Experts say the situation has worsened over time due to upstream deforestation, ageing embankments and heavier rainfall driven by climate change (google_news_india). Minister Pijush Hazarika described the current episode as the state's worst in six decades (google_news_india). On July 21 alone, 21 people died in 24 hours, most swept away or drowned, while others perished in landslides and house collapses (google_news_india). Around 40 people remain missing (google_news_india). The full scope of the damage and the final casualty count remain uncertain as waters continue to recede and access improves.
