## 4. Syrian Artist Turns to Belly Dancing Following Berlin Pride Attack

*Haidar Darwish channels grief into art after a deadly attack at Berlin's Pride festival left one person dead and dozens injured.*

> REPORT  ·  5 sources  ·  5 claim(s) kept  ·  4 verified

### Context

A gunman opened fire at Berlin's Pride festival, killing one person and wounding several others before fleeing the scene. The suspect was identified as Abdul Ballout, a German citizen with Lebanese roots who prosecutors said had previously sought to join the Islamic State group. After a nearly 24-hour manhunt, police located Ballout in the Spandau district on Sunday and shot and killed him during a confrontation.

### Report

Haidar Darwish stood on stage at Berlin’s Museum Island on Sunday night wearing a flamboyant hoop skirt and a red fez. Before the music started, the Syrian belly dancer and artist asked the audience for a moment of silence, then told the crowd the show must go on despite the fatal attack that had plunged the city’s LGBTQ+ community into mourning less than 24 hours earlier (ap_india).

“Yesterday there was an attack and a hate crime on our community in Berlin during the Pride Parade,” Darwish told the crowd, struggling to keep his emotions in check. “It’s heavy on our community but we are pushing through, and we are here specifically today, myself and my artists, to show up and be visible” (ap_india).

Saturday night’s van ramming and stabbing near the Christopher Street Day celebrations left one person dead and 29 injured. Authorities classified the violence as an extremist terror attack (ap_india). The suspect, identified as Abdul Ballout, a 21-year-old German citizen with Lebanese roots, was tracked down around 6 p.m. Sunday in an allotment garden complex in the Spandau district. Police said he ran toward officers with a sharp instrument; they fired at least one shot and he died at the scene (ap_india, indian_express).

Prosecutors said Ballout had previously sought to join the Islamic State group. He traveled to Lebanon in 2025 intending to reach Syria and made contact with people he believed were members of the militant group. A Lebanese military court sentenced him to three months’ imprisonment for offenses including incitement to religious and sectarian conflict. After returning to Germany, he was arrested at Berlin airport. In May, a juvenile court in Berlin convicted him of preparing a serious act of violence endangering the state and publishing Islamic State propaganda, handing down a suspended sentence of one year and ten months in youth custody that prosecutors had appealed (ap_india).

Darwish, who fled Syria in 2016 and performs as “The Darvish,” shared the stage with Queens Against Borders, a group created to build solidarity with LGBTQ+ refugees, migrants and immigrants. Also performing were Egyptian belly dancer Aleya and Nigerian drag performer Countess Sasha Seduction. Pushing aside the pain, Darwish began spinning like a traditional Anatolian dervish, his red skirt billowing in a graceful cone. Traditional Arabic melodies grew louder and faster as he stripped off the skirt and, to cheers, launched into belly dance moves (ap_india).

“We’re trying our best, to be honest, in these weird, horrible times to create a stage where we can be present,” the 30-year-old told The Associated Press. He stressed the importance of performing for a wider audience that included Berliners of all backgrounds and foreign tourists. “Nonetheless, we are tired to constantly be put in a position where we have to fight endlessly.” The dancers could easily stay home, he said, “but we can also put grief into joy” (ap_india).

By the end of the set, audience members had leaped from their chairs to join in, transforming an otherwise sad and somber night. Earlier Sunday, mourners including U.K. resident Ashley Jump had placed flowers at a memorial near the crime scene in Tiergarten park. “They’ll never erase us, we’ll never be gone,” Jump said. “As long as humanity has existed, we’ve existed. And as long as humanity exists, we will exist. We will not be removed” (ap_india).

The attack has reopened debate over how Ballout was able to move freely despite his conviction and known radicalization. Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt said the suspect had a history of criminal offenses and membership in the Islamist scene (indian_express). Chancellor Friedrich Merz, speaking ahead of a memorial service at St. Mary’s Church, urged residents to defend the country’s liberal values. “We will defend the freedom, openness, and liberal spirit of our lives and our society with everything we can do,” he said (indian_express). Questions remain over whether the suspended sentence and monitoring mechanisms were sufficient to prevent the violence.

## 6. Indian Banks Raise $32 Billion Through Dollar-Inflow Schemes, Says RBI Chief

*RBI Governor Sanjay Malhotra disclosed that Indian banks have mobilized $32 billion largely through FCNR(B) deposits and stated the central bank has adequate tools to manage liquidity.*

> REPORT  ·  5 sources  ·  9 claim(s) kept  ·  1 verified

### Context

Indian banks have raised $32 billion through special dollar-inflow schemes, a figure confirmed by Reserve Bank of India Governor Shaktikanta Das in an interview with Hindu Businessline. The fundraising comes as the central bank works to bolster foreign currency reserves and stabilize the rupee amid global financial volatility. These schemes are designed to encourage non-resident deposits and overseas borrowing by banks, providing a buffer against external shocks. The milestone underscores the banking sector's success in mobilizing foreign capital under the RBI's recent policy framework.

### Report

Reserve Bank of India Governor Sanjay Malhotra said Indian banks have mobilised nearly $32 billion through special dollar-inflow schemes, with the bulk of the funds raised via Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank) or FCNR(B) deposits (the_hindu). In an interview with The Hindu BusinessLine, Malhotra added that government securities have attracted more than $7 billion in foreign inflows since the policy measures were announced in June (the_hindu).

The Governor dismissed concerns that the inflows merely represent a recycling of existing deposits, stating that the central bank has adequate tools to manage any resulting liquidity (the_hindu). He said the measures have strengthened India’s external position amid heightened geopolitical uncertainty and volatile global capital flows (the_hindu).

Responding to questions about the RBI bearing the hedging cost of fresh FCNR(B) deposits and offering concessional foreign exchange swaps for external commercial borrowings by public sector entities, Malhotra said the arrangement poses no risk. “It is not something which should be a matter of concern because we have a foolproof system of insuring ourselves. So, whatever dollars we get, the excess foreign currency is invested in foreign assets. The risk, therefore, is not there,” he said (the_hindu).

On the rupee, Malhotra argued that recent depreciation does not reflect weakness in the country’s economic fundamentals. He attributed pressure on the currency to geopolitical tensions, dollar strength and broader volatility across emerging markets (the_hindu). “We do not target any specific exchange rate or band for the rupee. Our intervention, whenever necessary, is targeted to curb excessive volatility,” he said, adding that the currency is “not overvalued” and could even be considered undervalued in both nominal and real effective exchange rate terms (the_hindu).

Malhotra cited a current account surplus during April-May, robust services exports, resilient remittance inflows, rising merchandise exports and improving foreign direct investment flows as indicators of external sector strength (the_hindu). On reserve management, he said the RBI continues to be guided by the principles of safety, liquidity and returns, while reviewing reserve deployment periodically (the_hindu).

Turning to monetary policy, the Governor reiterated that inflation control remains the RBI’s foremost priority even as it remains mindful of growth risks. He said the Monetary Policy Committee will continue to adopt a data-dependent approach while navigating the evolving growth-inflation trade-off. “Our primary mandate is inflation and price stability. Therefore, we will do whatever is required first, to keep price stability and then, to see to what extent we can support growth,” he said (the_hindu).
